How to read this

Every finding below is tagged with one of five verdicts. They mean specifically:

Schema exists the column or table is already there — the ticket is a logic/UI/integration gap, not a schema gap New field an existing table needs one or more new columns New table no existing table can hold this — a new table (and usually a join) is needed Mid-migration the schema exists but is actively being replaced by a newer model in the same active backlog Covered elsewhere a different ticket or an already-shipped feature already answers this

Anything marked 🆕 NEW inside a diagram doesn't exist yet — it's a proposed table or column, grounded in a specific active ticket, not current schema. Everything else in the diagrams is real, verified schema. In full-screen, 🆕 NEW items are also highlighted in red so they stand out from a distance.

Dashed lines in the diagrams mean the two tables live in different systems and are linked only by a shared ID value that both sides agree to honor — there's no database rule forcing them to stay in sync, just convention (and in a few places, a background job that copies data across on a delay). Solid lines mean the two tables live in the same system and the link is a real, enforced database relationship.

Click any diagram to open it full-screen with pan and zoom — scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to re-center, Escape or the × button to close. Once you're in full-screen, click any connecting line to highlight it in blue so you can trace exactly which two tables it links, even when several lines cross near each other. Click the line again (or click empty space) to clear it. The diagrams are dense; this is the intended way to read them, not just the small inline view.

Three things stand out immediately. First, Events is running two systems at once — the old appointment table and a brand-new scheduling system, and Leads is already wired to both, mid-changeover. Second, Quotes doesn't live where the name suggests — it used to be its own separate system, but was recently absorbed into the same database as Orders. Third, almost every connection that crosses from one domain into another is the "different systems, matching ID only" kind, not a real enforced database link — which matters for reporting, because a join that works cleanly inside one system's data won't always work the same way once you're crossing between Leads, Quotes, Orders, and Work Orders.

RLN impact on Dealers: three new tables already exist and are live — one resolves a retail partner's store number (e.g. a specific Home Depot location) to the Brite dealer who services it, one records which dealers are teamed up to work that store's leads, and one is a simple on/off switch for whether a dealer participates in RLN at all. This is the foundation everything else in RLN routing sits on top of, and it already shipped (early July 2026) — it is not something still being built, even though it's easy to assume otherwise since it's so new. For your migration mapping: any "which dealer services this partner store" question should resolve through these three tables, not by inferring it from an address or account number.

Nothing in Dealers stores a direct pointer to a Lead, appointment, Quote, Order, Work Order, or Customer — those connections are always looked up live from the owning system when needed, not stored here. The one exception: when a dealer's SAP account number changes hands from one owner to another, Dealers actively pushes that change into the Orders system and keeps a record of exactly what got updated.

RLN impact on Customers: essentially none, and that's the useful thing to know. A homeowner who came in through Home Depot, JCPenney, or Costco Canada becomes a completely ordinary Customer record here — nothing on this table marks them as partner-sourced, and nothing needs to. The partner/channel identity lives entirely upstream, on the Lead that created them. For reporting and migration: if you ever need to segment customers by acquisition channel or retail partner, don't look for it here — you'll need to join back to the originating Lead and read its partner fields there. This table won't grow any new RLN-specific columns.

The connection from a Lead to a Customer record runs through the Leads side (leads_consumers), not this one. consumer_orders is the one direct link from a Customer straight to an Order, but it only gets filled in for the small slice of customers who buy directly from Hunter Douglas or Levolor with no dealer in between — for the much more common case (a dealer in the middle), the link runs from the Order side instead, via orders.brite_consumer_id.

RLN impact on Events: a full replacement scheduling system is being rolled out right now, live. Today, a customer's scheduled visit is a row in the old appointments table. None of the four scheduling models connect to Quotes or Orders directly. And the link from a Work Order to its scheduled visit still points at the old appointments table, not the new bookings table yet — that hasn't been switched over.

How this was built

Methodology & confidence

Seven parallel research passes mapped each domain directly from the database's own change history (every table and column ever added, renamed, or dropped) and cross-checked that against the application's own internal definitions and code. Three of those seven initial passes turned out to be working from an out-of-date copy of the codebase that pre-dated roughly a month of consolidation work — this was caught by directly checking specific claims (does this table actually exist, does this service still exist) against the current version, not assumed. The corrected facts driving the highest-confidence findings above — the RLN identity fields on leads, the partner_channel_mapping table, the new Scheduling Engine tables, the order-dealer-quote→order-status consolidation, and the install-work-order-assignment consolidation — were each verified by reading the actual migration file, not inferred from a summary.

248 active tickets (Backlog/Unstarted/Started/Triage, excluding Done/Cancelled/Duplicate) were pulled from the RLN-Discovery and RLN-Delivery Linear teams — there is no standalone "RDIS" team; RDIS is simply RLN-Discovery's issue-key prefix, unchanged since before the team was renamed. Every ticket named in a table above was checked against its own full Linear description and the live repository, not just its title — including three findings that came directly from that per-ticket pass rather than the initial schema sweep: services/doc-composer's real template/e-signature schema (missed entirely by the original domain mapping), confirmation that the RLN tax engine is still unmerged despite showing "In Review" in Linear, and the same-day pinnacle_escapes_rln_commissions table that resembles but isn't the missing dealer-commission ledger. Tickets referenced only in the "everything else" cluster or in a multi-ticket summary row were reviewed at the title and project level rather than individually re-verified against the schema.
